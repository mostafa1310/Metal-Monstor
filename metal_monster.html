Metal Monster V1 – Geared Shovel

Kit box 218 × 220 × 75 mm (15 mm panels, wheels outside) with a geared shovel (12T pinion → 43T sector on each side) at the front and an overhead hammer that hits front or back. Every motor is a GA25-370 12 V: 4 × 133 rpm drive (20 kg·cm), 2 × 170 rpm shovel, 1 × 170 rpm hammer (≈15.6 kg·cm, estimated). Other design: V2 (crank, simpler).

Numbers in the 3D view: 1 blade · 2 axle · 3 pinion + sector · 4 shovel motors · 5 coupling · 6 cheeks · 7–13 hammer · 14 drive. Use Zoom buttons + X-ray to see the motor mounts, couplings, bolts, gears and chain; Explode pulls the assemblies apart.

Shovel

Blade 30.9 × 13 cm (covers both front wheels), 6 mm thick at the hinge tapering to a sharp lip, side walls and hinge knuckles printed with it. It rests at 8.6° with the lip on the floor.

Hinge (both designs)

  • Dead axle: Ø8 steel rod, 327 mm, 20 mm above the floor, 20 mm in front of the kit box. It does not turn: shaft collars on both sides of each cheek hold it.
  • Cheek plates (6 mm ply, one inside each wall): 2 × M4 countersunk through the wall, washer + nyloc inside. Their front end is round (R13) around the axle so they slide over bumps.
  • Front extension plate (6 mm ply) on top of the kit base plate: 4 × M3. Carries the motor brackets.
  • Cheeks against the inside of the side panels; blade knuckles just outside them (x = 103–112 mm) and at the blade ends; rib/sector knuckles in the middle.

Motors (both designs)

  • 2 × GA25 170 rpm, one each side, 55 mm from the hinge at 112° (behind the blade when it is up). They fit inside the box width – no cutting of the side panels.
  • Printed L-bracket: 5 mm face plate (Ø8 hole for the Ø7 shaft boss; 6 mm of the 11.5 mm shaft reaches the coupler sleeve) + foot + gusset. GA25 face → plate with 2 × M3 countersunk. Foot → extension plate + kit base with 2 × M3 + nut.
  • Output: your 18 mm brass hex coupler on the D-shaft (2 set screws + Loctite). The pinion has a 12 mm hex socket, slides on the coupler hex and is held by an M4 screw + washer in the coupler's end thread.

Gears (V1)

Each GA25 (170 rpm) drives a 12-tooth pinion into its own 43-tooth sector gear (module 2, 3.58:1). Both sectors are bolted to the blade and turn on the axle. Stroke 84° (blade up to ≈75° from the floor) in ≈0.3 s no load / ≈0.5 s loaded. Stall torque at the blade ≈ 2 × 1.53 × 3.58 ≈ 11 N·m (estimate, design with half: ≈5.5 N·m). The printed teeth, not the motors, are now the weak point – see Check.

Control + limits

  • Stroke is limited to 84° because at the end of the stroke the sector tail passes 3 mm above the floor (slots in the base + extension plates let it through).
  • Hall sensor at rest + one at 84° (magnet on the sector). Reverse the motors to lower the blade.
  • Wire the two motors with opposite polarity – they face opposite directions.

Overhead hammer

PivotØ10 steel shaft, 15 cm high, 1.5 cm behind centre, in 2 × 6000-2RS bearings pressed into 9 mm ply uprights (printed retainer rings, 3 × M2.5).
Uprights9 mm ply, 29 mm each side of centre, each held by 2 + 2 aluminium angles (20×20×2), M4 through the lid and a 4 mm ply doubler underneath.
Arm2 × 9 mm ply, 22 cm to head centre, printed hub clamped to the shaft + M4 cross bolt through the shaft, 3 × M4 through-bolts, 2 printed spacers.
Head13 × 8 × 7 cm PETG 60 %, ply cheeks top/bottom, 2 × M4 vertical through-bolts, 5 mm TPU pads on both faces. Arm plates go 15 mm into slots in the head, 2 × M4.
DriveGA25 170 rpm → 18 mm brass hex coupler → printed 12T #25 sprocket with hex hub (M4 screw) → 56-link chain → 48T sprocket (4:1, 42 rpm and up to ≈6 N·m at the shaft, estimate).
Dog clutch48T sprocket turns free on a 10×14 bronze bush and carries a Ø5 dog pin. A Ø36 disc clamped to the shaft carries the other Ø5 pin. They touch at 22°. The motor can only push; the hammer is free to run ahead.
EnergyBand crank (32 mm) on the left end of the shaft, 2–4 bands to an anchor post on the lid. Longest at the top (over-centre) ⇒ they throw the hammer down on either side. ≈1.5–2 J at the head.
StopsRear 70° and front 284°: printed posts with TPU pads, catching the arm ≈9 cm from the pivot.

One front hit, step by step

  1. Rest at 70° on the rear stop, bands pull it down onto the stop. Dog pin touching the disc pin.
  2. Motor forward ≈ 0.5 s no load, ≈1–1.5 s with the bands: dog pushes the hammer to just past the top (≈186°), bands stretched.
  3. Motor stops. Bands + gravity throw the hammer forward; it leaves the dog behind. Hit at ≈275° (or front stop at 284°).
  4. Re-arm: motor reverses ≈220° until the dog touches the other side of the pin, pushes the hammer back over the top, it drops onto the rear stop. Motor goes forward ≈210° to be ready again.

For a rear hit: same, but first put the hammer on the front stop, then push it back over the top.

Hall sensor on the right upright + magnet on the disc = "hammer at rest". Motor current limit ≈ 3 A; never let the motor push the hammer against a stop.

Drive & frame

  • Kit box: two 220×75×15 side panels with the 188×75×15 rear panel between them (outside 218 × 220, inside 188 × 190). Front panel left off. Red lid (front 4 cm open), plywood base. Wheels Ø85 outside the side panels, 11.5 mm gap for the coupler sleeve.
  • Drive motor mount: Ø26 pocket 11 mm deep from the inside of the wall (4 mm web left), Ø8 hole for the shaft boss, 2 × M3×8 countersunk from outside into the gearbox face.
  • Wheel: 18 mm brass hex coupler – sleeve on the motor shaft (set screws + Loctite), 12 mm hex in the wheel hub, M4 screw + washer from outside into the coupler thread.
  • Speed ≈ 0.59 m/s (133 rpm, Ø85).
  • Lid: 6 × M3 into the side-panel tops (heat-set inserts). 4 mm plywood doubler underneath spreads the hammer loads.
  • Battery 3S between the motors on the base, controller board above it, receiver behind.

Mechanical check

Problems found in the earlier model and how this version solves them, then things to verify on the real parts.

CRIT ✔ fixed in model
Hammer catapult back-drove the GA25
After the top, the bands must spin the hammer faster than the motor. Through a 4:1 chain and a ~1:34 gearbox the GA25 would be dragged at >15 000 rpm: it acts as a brake and the gearbox teeth take the shock.
→ Dog clutch: the 48T sprocket turns free on a bronze bush and only pushes a pin on a disc keyed to the shaft. Past the top the hammer runs away from the pin. To re-arm, the motor turns the other way around (≈220°) and pushes from the other side.
CRIT ✔ fixed in model
GA25 shaft (11.5 mm) barely longer than the 15 mm side panel
Mounted on the inside face, the shaft cannot reach the wheel.
→ Ø26 mm pocket, 11 mm deep, from the inside of each side panel (4 mm web left). Motor face bolts to the web with 2 × M3 countersunk. Pocket 12 mm deep (3 mm web): the shaft sticks out 8.5 mm, the coupler sleeve grips 8 mm of it (set screws on the D-flat) and its hex goes into the wheel. Gap between the two motors: 10 cm (room for battery + electronics).
HIGH ✔ fixed in model
New kit size: 218 × 220 box with the wheels outside
Total width at the wheels becomes ≈29.1 cm (wheels sit 11.5 mm out for the coupler sleeve). A 24 cm blade would have its side walls swing back into the front wheels when it lifts.
→ Blade widened to 30.9 cm so it covers both wheels (also protects them); axle lengthened. Change wheelW in lib/robot.js once you measure the wheel.
MED ✔ fixed in model

The cheeks, axle and shovel motors sit where the 188 mm front panel would be.
→ Leave the front panel off (keep it as a spare / for the extension plate).

→ Clash sweep: the head first touches the sector gears at 287°. Front stop set to 284°. Opponent tops ≥ 4.9 cm high are hit before the stop.

MED ⚠ check when building
Measure your GA25 before printing
Face screw spacing (≈17 mm, 2 × M3), shaft length (9–12 mm), D-flat.
→ Change bracket holes if different. Loctite 243 on every set screw on a D-shaft.
MED ⚠ check when building
Coupler on the Ø4 shaft
Your method: 2 set screws lock the shaft, an M4 screw in the front thread holds the gear / crank / sprocket on the 12 mm hex – this is what the model shows.
→ If the coupler bore is 6 mm on the 4 mm shaft, the set screws push the shaft to one side (up to 1 mm off-centre) and the gear wobbles. A 4→6 mm brass sleeve (or a 4 mm bore coupler) keeps it centred. Loctite 243 on the set screws, one on the D-flat.
HIGH ⚠ check when building
The motors are stronger than first assumed
Drive listing: 20 kg·cm at 133 rpm ⇒ the 170 rpm weapon motors ≈15.6 kg·cm (1.53 N·m) – about 3× my first estimate. Good for flipping, but the printed parts see more load: V1 pinion tooth ≈130 N, printed 12T sprocket ≈125 N chain pull, 1.5 N·m on every hex socket.
→ V1: print pinions + sectors in PA-CF/PA 100 %, or limit motor current. Hammer: PA 12T sprocket + spares. Motor face plates are 5 mm. Driver ≥ 3 A per motor; check the stall current on the 170 rpm listing and set a current limit so a stall never lasts more than ~1 s.
HIGH ✔ fixed in model
How the coupler sets the spacing
Sleeve 11 mm + hex 7 mm. The GA25 shaft is 11.5 mm from the face (incl. the 2.5 mm boss), D-flat on the outer 8 mm.
→ Motor face plates are 5 mm (3 mm web on the side panels) → 6–8 mm of shaft inside the sleeve and both set screws land on the D-flat. Wheels sit 11.5 mm off the panels (sleeve in the gap, hex in the wheel hub). Every driven part has a 12 mm hex socket and is held by an M4 screw into the coupler end.
MED ⚠ check when building
Printed parts on the hex
Hex sockets in printed parts wear and crack at the corners.
→ Print 100 % infill, socket 12.1–12.2 mm across flats, test-fit first; print spares of the crank / pinion and the 12T sprocket.
MED ⚠ check when building
Band force
For ≈1.5–2 J you need ≈50 N average band force. The anchor post and band pin see this all the time.
→ Start with 2 bands; add until the hammer still reaches the top. Anchor post through-bolted into the doubler.
