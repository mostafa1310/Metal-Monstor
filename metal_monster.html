Metal Monster

Kit box 179 × 220 × 75 mm (15 mm panels, Ø86 × 39 wheels outside, 263 mm over the tyres) with a crank-and-rod shovel (no gears, one motor turn = one flip) at the front and an overhead hammer that hits front or back. Every motor is a GA25-370 12 V: 4 × 133 rpm drive (20 kg·cm), 2 × 170 rpm shovel, 1 × 170 rpm hammer (≈15.6 kg·cm, estimated).

Numbers in the 3D view: 1 blade · 2 axle · 3 crank + rod · 4 shovel motors · 5 coupling · 6 cheeks · 7–13 hammer · 14 drive. Use Zoom buttons + X-ray to see the motor mounts, couplings, bolts, gears and chain; Explode pulls the assemblies apart.

Shovel

Blade 26 × 13 cm (= the 260 mm printer bed), 6 mm thick at the hinge tapering to a sharp lip, hinge knuckles printed with it. No side walls: a robot wider than 26 cm still slides onto the whole blade face instead of riding up on the wall edges. It rests at 8.6° with the lip on the floor.

Hinge (both designs)

  • Dead axle: Ø8 steel rod, 260 mm, 20 mm above the ground, 20 mm in front of the kit box. It does not turn: shaft collars on both sides of each cheek hold it.
  • Cheek plates (6 mm ply, one inside each wall): 2 × M4 countersunk through the wall, washer + nyloc inside. Their front end is round (R13) around the axle so they slide over bumps.
  • Front extension plate (6 mm ply) on top of the kit base plate: 4 × M3. Carries the motor brackets.
  • Cheeks against the inside of the side panels; blade knuckles just outside them (x = 84–92 mm) and 8 mm in from the blade ends, with the outer shaft collars tucked under the blade so the 260 mm axle ends flush; rib knuckles in the middle.

Motors (both designs)

  • 2 × GA25 170 rpm, one each side, 55 mm from the hinge at 112° (behind the blade when it is up). They fit inside the box width – no cutting of the side panels.
  • Printed L-bracket: 5 mm face plate (Ø8 hole for the Ø7 shaft boss; 6 mm of the 11.5 mm shaft reaches the coupler sleeve) + foot + gusset. GA25 face → plate with 2 × M3 countersunk. Foot → extension plate + kit base with 2 × M3 + nut.
  • Output: your 18 mm brass hex coupler on the D-shaft (2 set screws + Loctite). The crank has a 12 mm hex socket, slides on the coupler hex and is held by an M4×12 countersunk screw in the coupler's end thread (the head sits in a countersink in the crank's end wall and clamps it onto the coupler).

Crank + rod

No gears. Each GA25 (170 rpm) turns a 37.5 mm crank. A 55 mm rod pulls a lug on the blade rib, 60 mm from the hinge (14° above the blade line). One full motor turn = one flip: the blade lifts 90° (to ≈82° from the floor) and comes back down, driven both ways. The lift uses 240° of the crank ⇒ ≈0.23 s at 170 rpm (no load). The linkage multiplies the motor torque by ≥ 1.6× ⇒ blade stall torque ≥ 4.9 N·m (estimate, design with half: ≈2.4 N·m) – enough to lift the front of a 3 kg robot.

Control

  • Hall sensor on the bracket + magnet on the crank = "home". Flip button: run until the magnet comes back (one turn). No reversing.
  • Wire the two motors with opposite polarity – they face opposite directions. Test with the rods off: both cranks must turn the same way (seen from the side).
  • Mount both cranks at the same angle when the blade is flat (use the magnet as reference).

Overhead hammer (simple stick)

One wooden stick with a small head that sticks out front and back – it hits a robot in front or behind. The hub sits straight on the motor's brass hex coupler, like the shovel crank – the motor shaft is the pivot (no shaft, bearings, chain or gears). Light on purpose: less mass = the motor swings it faster. The chain / gear versions are still in the Hammer drive tab if you ever want them; the 3D model follows that choice.

One hit, step by step

  1. Front: hammer rests on the rear stop. Full power forward: it goes over the top and down onto the robot in front. Brake, then half power back to the rear stop.
  2. Rear: half power forward to the front stop, then full power backwards over the top onto the robot behind. Then back to the rear stop.
The motor is always connected to the hammer now: it must never push against a stop. Hall sensor on the upright + magnet on the hub = "home". Current limit ≈ 3 A, cut power if stalled more than ≈ 1 s. Brake (short the motor) before a stop instead of coasting into it.

Hammer drive – direct, chain or gears

Direct on motor is the chosen design: fewest parts, and the fastest hit. Chain and gears stay here to compare. Pick one below – the 3D model is rebuilt with it (sprocket / gear sizes, chain length, motor position, bracket) and the numbers update. ▶ Hit front / Hit rear play the swing at the speed the motor really gives with that pair. Click any row of the compare table to try it.

Why a low ratio for this hammer

The stick hammer weighs only ≈ 80 g, so the motor has torque to spare even at 1 : 1. A bigger ratio only makes it slower – the hit energy grows with speed², so 1 : 1 hits hardest – and 1 : 1 needs no chain or gears at all: the hammer goes straight on the motor. Use 2 : 1 or more only if you make the head heavier (try the head-mass box).

How to check what you have

  • Chain pitch = pin to pin: 6.35 mm = #25 / 04C · 8 mm = 05B · 9.525 mm = #35 / 06B · 12.7 mm = #40 / 08B / bicycle. Measure 10 pitches and divide by 10.
  • Sprocket: count the teeth, measure the outside Ø and compare with the table. The tooth width must fit inside the chain (≈ 2.8 mm for #25).
  • Gear: module = outside Ø ÷ (teeth + 2). Both gears must have the same module. Printed gears here are module 2.
  • Different pitch or a pair not in the list? Send the pitch + tooth counts and it gets added.

Mechanical check

Problems found in the earlier model and how this version solves them, then things to verify on the real parts.

Hammer redesigned (direct drive, wooden stick). The cards below about rubber bands, the dog clutch and the 48T sprocket are about the old hammer and no longer apply. New: every chain and gear option in the Hammer drive tab was swept with the clash check – 0 clashes; the chain length solver keeps the motor sprocket above the lid. Without the front stop the head would touch the shovel rods at 294° (stop at 284°).
CRIT ✔ fixed in model
Hammer catapult back-drove the GA25
After the top, the bands must spin the hammer faster than the motor. Through a 4:1 chain and a ~1:34 gearbox the GA25 would be dragged at >15 000 rpm: it acts as a brake and the gearbox teeth take the shock.
→ Dog clutch: the 48T sprocket turns free on a bronze bush and only pushes a pin on a disc keyed to the shaft. Past the top the hammer runs away from the pin. To re-arm, the motor turns the other way around (≈220°) and pushes from the other side.
CRIT ✔ fixed in model
GA25 shaft (11.5 mm) barely longer than the 15 mm side panel
Mounted on the inside face, the shaft cannot reach the wheel.
→ Ø26 mm pocket, 11 mm deep, from the inside of each side panel (4 mm web left). Motor face bolts to the web with 2 × M3 countersunk. Pocket 12 mm deep (3 mm web): the shaft sticks out 8.5 mm, the coupler sleeve grips 8 mm of it (set screws on the D-flat) and its hex goes into the wheel. Gap between the two motors: 10 cm (room for battery + electronics).
HIGH ✔ fixed in model
New kit size: 179 × 220 box, Ø86 × 39 wheels, 263 mm over the tyres
The wheels sit 3 mm off the panels with the coupler recessed in the hub. A blade narrower than the tyres would have its side walls swing back into the front wheels when it lifts, and the narrower box puts the shovel motors through the side panels.
→ Blade 260 mm (printer bed) with no side walls, so nothing swings into the wheels; axle 260 mm, collars inside the blade ends. U-notch in the front top of each side panel for the shovel motor.
MED ✔ fixed in model
Kit front panel
The cheeks, axle and shovel motors sit where the 188 mm front panel would be.
→ Leave the front panel off (keep it as a spare / for the extension plate).
CRIT ✔ fixed in model

MED ⚠ check when building
Coupler on the Ø4 shaft
Your method: 2 set screws lock the shaft, an M4 screw in the front thread holds the gear / crank / sprocket on the 12 mm hex – this is what the model shows.
→ If the coupler bore is 6 mm on the 4 mm shaft, the set screws push the shaft to one side (up to 1 mm off-centre) and the gear wobbles. A 4→6 mm brass sleeve (or a 4 mm bore coupler) keeps it centred. Loctite 243 on the set screws, one on the D-flat.
HIGH ⚠ check when building
The motors are stronger than first assumed
Drive listing: 20 kg·cm at 133 rpm ⇒ the 170 rpm weapon motors ≈15.6 kg·cm (1.53 N·m) – about 3× my first estimate. Good for flipping, but the printed parts see more load: printed 12T sprocket ≈125 N chain pull, 1.5 N·m on every hex socket.
→ Print the cranks at 100 % and limit motor current. Hammer: PA 12T sprocket + spares. Motor face plates are 5 mm. Driver ≥ 3 A per motor; check the stall current on the 170 rpm listing and set a current limit so a stall never lasts more than ~1 s.
HIGH ✔ fixed in model
How the coupler sets the spacing
Sleeve 11 mm + hex 7 mm. The GA25 shaft is 11.5 mm from the face (incl. the 2.5 mm boss), D-flat on the outer 8 mm.
→ Motor face plates are 5 mm (3 mm web on the side panels) → 6–8 mm of shaft inside the sleeve and both set screws land on the D-flat. Wheels sit 3 mm off the panels (coupler recessed in the wheel hub). Every driven part has a 12 mm hex socket and is held by an M4 screw into the coupler end.
